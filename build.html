01 · Introduction

Mobility, where the pavement ends.

People with lower-limb disabilities often lack mobility options outside of paved environments. Handcycles offer an alternative to bicycles — independence and freedom of movement — but capable off-road models with integrated power assist are prohibitively expensive.

ELEOS Solutions sponsored a senior design team at George Fox University to make effective handcycles more accessible to the people who need them.

02 · Problem Statement

The gap.

Current off-road handcycles with integrated power assist are prohibitively expensive — leaving many individuals, especially in low- and middle-income regions, without access to durable mobility solutions. Existing assistive devices often fail on rugged terrain such as dirt, mud, or rocky paths, limiting opportunity. This project designs and prototypes a recumbent off-road handcycle that is affordable, adaptable, and capable, incorporating power assist with hybrid propulsion.

03 · Design Details

Six subsystems. One ride.

Designed around accessible fabrication and standard bike parts, so future teams can iterate without starting from scratch.

01

Frame

Welded T-6061 aluminum square tube — the same alloy used in performance bikes. Strong, lightweight, and forgiving to fabricate. Mounts the seat, leg slings, chain tensioner, and routing sprockets directly.

T-6061 Al Welded
02

Steering

A second set of handlebars on the steering column adds leverage for hard turns and mounts the brakes, shifter, and e-assist controls. Geometry tuned for low-effort direct steering with auto-centering wheels and minimal bump-steer.

Auto-centering Direct steer
03

Suspension

Front and rear. The front uses a double-wishbone system — two arms connecting wheel to frame, supported by a center shock — well suited to absorb the large bumps of off-road riding. The rear runs a traditional bike-style shock.

Double wishbone Front + rear
04

Wheel Mounts

Single-side tricycle wheels make tire changes simple. The axle runs through the kingpin — a cylinder linking wheels to suspension and letting them pivot when steered. Tie rods drive a lever arm that also carries the disc-brake mounts. Rear is a standard bike quick-release.

Kingpin pivot Disc brakes
05

Drivetrain

Three sections: front transfers torque from the hand cranks back; the e-assist adds motor torque to that input; the rear delivers it through a 1×10 mountain-bike gear system to the rear wheel. Result: a vehicle that handles rough ground and hills on hand-crank or throttle alone.

1×10 MTB gearing 3-stage
06

E-Assist

A 750 W motor and battery give the rider ample assist for long rides. An on-bar screen shows battery life, speed, and pedal-assist mode — letting the rider engage the motor on demand for hills and long distances.

750 W motor Pedal-assist modes
04 · Conclusion

Functional. Significantly cheaper. Iteratable.

The fully-functional prototype was built using welding, milling, and waterjet cutting, with a final material cost of ~$3,050. While that price point is still inaccessible to many, it is significantly cheaper than existing solutions on the market — and further optimization may reduce cost while improving performance.

A senior design team next year will take this design and iterate on it — testing its limits and optimizing it toward the goal of a durable, affordable, and capable off-road handcycle.
